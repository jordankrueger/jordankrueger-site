Copy-paste AI prompts for the start-on-your-own tasks.

These are for the three items you can run without waiting on me. Open any AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, whatever you like), paste a prompt, and fill in the highlighted bits. Each one tells the AI exactly what to do so you don't have to figure out the format.

How to use these

  1. Copy a whole prompt block (everything in the dark box).
  2. Paste it into your AI assistant.
  3. Replace anything highlighted in yellow with the real info, then send.
  4. The output goes into the shared tracker / digest. That's the proof the work happened.

One rule: if the AI gives you a number, a dollar amount, or a link you can't see in a real source, don't trust it — double-check or drop it. AI sometimes makes things up.

Task 1

Foundation grant announcements

Weekly: scan grant news, send Jordan a short digest of relevant orgs.

Proof → weekly digest email

1One-time setup — find the sources to watch

I'm tracking grant announcements for progressive and advocacy nonprofits
in the United States. Help me build a starter list of the best free
sources to monitor each week — newsletters, websites, RSS feeds, and
email alerts. For each one, give me: the name, what it covers, how to
subscribe or where to check, and whether it's free. Focus on sources
that report when advocacy / progressive nonprofits receive notable
funding. Format it as a simple list I can work through.

Run this once. Subscribe to the good ones, then use prompt #2 every week.

2Weekly — turn what you found into Jordan's digest

Below is grant news I collected this week. Pull out ONLY the items that
fit all of these: (a) the recipient is a progressive or advocacy
nonprofit, (b) it's a US org, (c) the funding is notable (roughly six
figures or more, or otherwise a big deal for them).

For each one that qualifies, give me one row in this exact format:
- Org name | Grant amount | Funder | Source link | One sentence on why it's
  relevant to a digital/IT consultant (e.g. "midsize org, likely to invest
  in their website next quarter").

If something doesn't clearly qualify, leave it out. Don't invent amounts
or links — only use what's in the text I'm pasting. If an amount isn't
stated, write "amount not stated."

HERE'S THIS WEEK'S NEWS:
[paste the headlines / newsletter text / articles you found]

Paste the result into the weekly digest email to Jordan. Done.

Task 2

Outdated-stack scans of nonprofit sites

Run sites through BuiltWith/Wappalyzer, log what's stale in a shared sheet.

Proof → shared Google Sheet

1Make sense of one scan + flag what's stale

I ran a nonprofit's website through BuiltWith (a tool that shows what
software a site is built on). Below is the raw output. Turn it into one
clean spreadsheet row with these columns:

Org name | Website URL | Platform (e.g. WordPress) | Version (if shown) |
Theme/builder | Other notable tech | Red flags

Under "Red flags," call out anything that looks outdated or risky — an old
WordPress version, an abandoned theme, end-of-life PHP, no SSL, page
builders known to be heavy/insecure. If nothing's stale, write "looks
current." Keep it short and factual — don't guess at things the scan
doesn't show.

ORG: [org name]
URL: [website url]
BUILTWITH OUTPUT:
[paste what BuiltWith / Wappalyzer showed]

Copy the row into the shared sheet. Repeat per site.

2Optional — translate the tech jargon into plain English

For the "Red flags" I just logged, write me one plain-English sentence
per flag explaining why it matters to a nonprofit — the kind of thing
that would make sense to someone non-technical. No fearmongering, just
clear. Here are the flags:
[paste the red flags from the row]

Useful later if Jordan wants a "here's why your site needs attention" note. Skip if you're moving fast.

Task 3 · bonus

Directory & listing submissions

Once Jordan hands you the listing copy, these keep every submission consistent.

Proof → live listing URL per directory

1Fit Jordan's blurb to each directory's form

I'm submitting a consulting business to an online directory. Here's the
directory's form fields and character limits, and here's the standard
blurb. Rewrite the blurb to fit each field cleanly — respect every
character limit, keep the meaning and tone, don't add claims that aren't
in the original. Give me the final text field-by-field, ready to paste.

DIRECTORY FIELDS / LIMITS:
[paste the form fields, e.g. "Tagline (80 char), Description (300 char), Services (pick 3)"]

STANDARD BLURB JORDAN GAVE ME:
[paste Jordan's master description]

Submit, then drop the live listing URL in the tracker.

If you get stuck

These prompts do the formatting and the thinking-out-loud — your job is the legwork (collecting the news, running the scans, hitting submit) and a quick sanity check on what the AI gives back.

Anything unclear, ask Jordan to walk through one live. After that you're on your own and the proof shows up in the tracker either way.
