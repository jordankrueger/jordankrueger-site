There's a popular worry about AI right now: that it flattens everything. Same essays, same logos, same bland "delve into" prose. Everyone using the same tool, so everything comes out the same.

I get why people say it. I've seen plenty of bland AI output too. But that hasn't been my experience at all. For me, AI hasn't made my work look like everyone else's. It's let me make things that didn't exist before, ideas I've had for years and never had the time, money, or skills to build alone.

The sameness critique assumes AI is replacing what you would have made anyway. In my case, the alternative wasn't a more original version. The alternative was nothing.

## The ideas were always mine

Last week I wrote about [why AI works for my ADHD brain](/blog/ai-is-my-crutch/): it gets me through the long, dull middle of a project. This post is about what comes out the other end.

Sameness happens when AI supplies the idea. That's not how I use it. What I bring is oddly particular: a Star Trek community I've run since 1994, a merged list of bad email domains, a scorecard for one CEO's predictions. You can't average your way to any of those.

AI didn't choose what to build. It made it practical for one person to build all of it.

## What I've already made

Here's a partial list of things that exist now that didn't a year ago. None of them look like each other, and none of them look like anyone else's.

- **[Job Seeker's Union](https://jobseekersunion.org)**: an advocacy group treating ghost jobs and hiring silence as collective problems, with the [Transparent Hiring Pledge](https://hiringpledge.com/), [Four Days with AI](https://fourdayswithai.com/), and a free layoff resource center.
- **[Equity Language Commons](https://equitylanguagecommons.org)**: dozens of equity language guides cross-referenced term by term, so you can see where they disagree.
- **[Freehold](https://freehold.is)**: a whitepaper for an internet where you own what you publish.
- **[Awesome ActionKit](https://github.com/jordankrueger/awesome-actionkit)**: the hub of ActionKit tools and recipes I wish I'd had when I started.
- **[Progressive email suppression list](https://github.com/jordankrueger/progressive-email-suppression)**: about 66,000 bad domains, merged from lists orgs have quietly passed around for years, rebuilt nightly.
- **[Claude Code guardrails](https://github.com/jordankrueger/backup-first-ai)**: backup-first safety defaults for nonprofit staff letting an AI agent loose on their laptop.
- **[AI Progress Tracker](https://aiprogresstracker.com/)**: a sourced scorecard for the predictions in "Machines of Loving Grace."
- **[HighSpeedRail.TV](https://highspeedrail.tv)**, **[User Manuals](https://usermanuals.guide/)**, **[Ready for AOC](https://readyforaoc.com)**, and **[Progressives for AI](https://progressivesforai.com/)**.
- Small tools: a [petition delivery formatter](https://github.com/jordankrueger/petition-delivery-formatter), a [lease mileage tracker](https://jordankrueger.github.io/lease-tracker/), and a [Matrix gatekeeper bot](https://github.com/jordankrueger/matrix-gatekeeper).

And that's just what's public. It doesn't count the infrastructure behind [StarBase 118](https://www.starbase118.net), the client work, or the creative works that AI has helped me develop.

## What's coming next

The next set is the one I'm most excited about, and it's the clearest case for what AI makes possible. These aren't apps. They're the kind of public-interest work that used to take a think tank, a staff, and a foundation grant.

The common thread: this administration has gutted much of the federal government. Someone needs to be drafting what comes next, in the open, where anyone can read it and make it better.

- **A plan for how government should run**: open, public-domain documents for how agencies could work better, written so experts spend their time improving drafts instead of starting from a blank page. AI makes it possible to draft and organize that much material at all.
- **Shared civic research**: what actually works in government, sourced and citable. AI does the first pass of reading and organizing, and I check it.
- **Local civic plumbing**: making it easier for community groups to get their information everywhere it needs to go. That's the boring connective work AI is good at.
- **Open community spaces**: places for groups to organize online without being locked inside a big platform.

None of these are finished, and I'll share details when they are. But the point stands now. A few years ago, any one of these would have been a full-time job I'd never have started. Now one person can credibly start all of them. That's not AI making everything the same. That's AI making civic infrastructure something a regular person can build.

## Sameness is a choice

If you ask AI to write the thing everyone else is writing, you'll get the thing everyone else is writing. That's real. But it's a choice about how you use the tool, not a law of nature.

Use it to build the thing only you would think of, and you get the opposite: more variety in the world, not less.
