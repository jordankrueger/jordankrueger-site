I'm hopeful about AI. I want it built well, and I want its benefits shared with everyone. That's the short version. Here's the longer one.

My day job is helping progressive nonprofits with their technology, so I spend a lot of time thinking about what small teams could do with better tools. AI is the biggest jump in that kind of capacity I've ever seen. I think it's our best shot in my lifetime at ending poverty, disease, loneliness and war. That's a big part of why I wrote down [what I want America to look like at 300](/2076).

This idea has roots on the left. In 2013, Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams argued that progressives should stop resisting new technology and put it to work for people. Aaron Bastani later imagined automation and clean energy ending scarcity for everyone. Some people call this "left accelerationism." I agree with them about where we should be headed. Where I part ways with the louder tech-world version is the question of who all this is for.

Speed alone doesn't decide who benefits. Here's what I want:

- **A public stake.** The public should hold a real ownership stake in the most powerful AI companies, the kind Bernie Sanders proposed this year, and the gains should go back to every person as a dividend.
- **Universal services.** Healthcare, housing and education shouldn't depend on whether your job survived automation.
- **A shorter work week at the same pay** as productivity climbs.
- **The tools in the right hands.** Workers, organizers and the people who usually get new technology last should get it first.

There are real ways this goes wrong. If a handful of companies and billionaires keep control of AI, we get today's inequality, only faster. If people lose their jobs before there's a safety net, they pay for progress they don't share in. And the same systems that could cure diseases can be used to watch and control people. That's why democratic oversight matters as much as the code.

Plenty of people on the left want to slow AI down or stop it. I understand why: they've watched technology get used against workers before, and they're right about the risks. I just don't think stopping it is possible, or even desirable. Trying to slow it down hands the future to whoever keeps building. I'd rather we shape it.

That's what being an optimist with a plan means to me. I'm not betting on AI to save us. I'm betting on people who decide what it's for.
